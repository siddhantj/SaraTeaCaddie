Why it tastes different

I buy from the garden, not from a warehouse.

Most Japanese tea that reaches America is blended for consistency and stored for years. Mine is chosen lot by lot in the growing regions, shipped cold, and sold while it still tastes like the spring it came from.

That is the whole business. It is why I keep it small, and why I would rather talk with you about what you pour than send you a price list.

Six teas to know first

Sencha

Bright, grassy, faintly sweet. Twenty-two of them in the caddie.
Gyokuro

Shaded three weeks. Thick, savoury, almost broth-like.
Matcha

Stone-milled shade-grown leaf, whisked whole into water.
Hojicha

Roasted to amber. Toasty, gentle, very little caffeine.
Genmaicha

Green tea with roasted rice. Comforting and popcorn-sweet.
Kukicha

Stems and stalks. Light, creamy, quietly sweet.
