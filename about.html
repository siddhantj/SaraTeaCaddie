About Sara

I have been carrying tea
between two countries
for twenty-eight years.

Portrait of Sara Kadowaki

My name is Sara Kadowaki. I founded Sara’s Tea Caddie in New York in 1998, for one reason: the Japanese tea I could find here tasted nothing like the tea I grew up drinking.

Good Japanese tea is agricultural and seasonal. It has a harvest date, a garden, a cultivar and a maker, and it fades. What was arriving in America was blended flat and held too long, and so a whole category of extraordinary flavour was simply missing from the country.

So I went back to the growers. Every year I travel through twelve growing regions across six prefectures — Shizuoka, Kyoto, Kagoshima, Fukuoka, Mie and Saitama — and taste that season’s lots at the source. I buy small quantities from makers I know by name, and I bring back fifty-one teas: twenty-two senchas alone, plus gyokuro, matcha, hojicha, genmaicha, kukicha and the rest.

In New York I keep them cold and move them quickly. My customers are cafés, restaurants, hotels and shops who care what is in the cup, and my job is less selling than matching: your water, your equipment, your menu, your guests. That is a conversation, not a checkout.

I still taste every lot myself before it ships. If a tea is not something I would happily pour for a friend, it does not go in the caddie.

The work, in order
Spring I fly to Japan for the first flush and taste the new season’s lots beside the people who made them.
Buying Small lots, single gardens, named cultivars. Nothing blended to hit a price.
Shipping Cold, sealed and prompt — the two things that decide whether green tea survives the journey.
New York Stored cold in Midtown, released in small quantities so nothing sits and dulls.
Your bar We taste together, work out water and timing, and train whoever will be brewing it.
